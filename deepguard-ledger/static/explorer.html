Ledger Explorer

Every block of the ledger, drawn in order. Each one stores the hash of the block before it — the arrows — so changing anything in an old block breaks the arrow after it. Press Re-check in my browser and this page recomputes every hash, Merkle root and signature itself with your browser's own cryptography; it does not take the server's word for anything.

Witnesses

Two independent copy-keepers. Each one checked every block when it arrived, wrote its hash in its own notebook and signed it. Their notebooks are fetched straight from your browser, not through the Deep-Guard server, and compared with the chain above. If Deep-Guard ever rewrote history — even with its real key — the witnesses would disagree.

Tamper lab

Try to cheat. Pick an attack and a block; it runs on a copy of the chain (your real files are not touched) and the copy is checked exactly like the real one. For the real thing, open deepguard-ledger/ledger_data/chain.jsonl in VS Code, change one letter in an old block, save, and press Re-check in my browser. Undo the change to make it green again.

Check a receipt

A receipt is a small JSON file you can download for any sealed record. It holds the record, its Merkle path, the block header and the signatures — enough to prove the record is on the ledger without the ledger. Drop one here to check it in your browser, or offline with python tools/verify_ledger.py receipt <file> <public key>.

Creators & keys

Everyone who has filed a record, with the short ID of their public key. Deep-Guard's own key is the one that signs every block.
