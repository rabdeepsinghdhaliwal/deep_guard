Crop & edit-robust matching

Content Registry

The Authenticity Fingerprint on the main page proves “is this the exact same file” and “does this still look the same after recompression.” Neither one survives a crop — the perceptual hash there is a description of the whole frame, and cropping it breaks the comparison almost immediately. This page answers a different, harder question: is this a crop, edit, or partial reuse of something already registered? That's the actual mechanism behind stopping someone from cropping a signature off a registered artwork and reselling it as new.

It also shows its work. Every upload gets its distinct features — the distinctive points the registry recognises it by, what each part of it looks like, its colours — and every match says what the two images have in common, how many distinctive points confirm it, what was done to the copy, and how much of the score each shared part is responsible for.

Registering stores a signed fingerprint record, a content-similarity vector and the image's distinctive points (positions and short descriptions, plus an 8×8 grid of average colours) — never the image itself.
